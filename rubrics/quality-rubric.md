# Quality Rubric

This document defines minimum criteria to evaluate the quality of outputs generated by agents.

The goal is not to maximize the volume of information, but to ensure:

- operational utility
- adherence to evidence
- analytical discipline
- scope control

---

## 1. Clarity and Structure

The response must:

- be organized into clear sections
- avoid long, poorly structured blocks
- prioritize relevant points (max. 5 per section)
- avoid redundancy

---

## 2. Adherence to Evidence

The response must:

- base statements on observable data
- avoid categorical claims without support
- make clear when something is:
  - fact
  - inference
  - hypothesis

Avoid:

- extrapolation
- gap-filling with assumptions

---

## 3. Analytical Discipline

The response must:

- clearly separate:
  - confirmed facts
  - hypotheses
  - gaps (unknowns)
- acknowledge collection limitations
- avoid premature conclusions

---

## 4. Operational Usefulness

The response must:

- address the objective of the spec
- prioritize actionable information
- avoid irrelevant “curiosities”
- indicate next steps when applicable

---

## 5. Scope Control

The response must:

- respect the scope defined in the spec
- avoid unauthorized pivots
- not investigate natural persons outside the scope

---

## 6. Signs of Low Quality

Output is considered weak when it presents:

- excessive text without useful information
- conclusions without evidence
- mixing fact and opinion
- absence of gaps or uncertainties
- vague language ("probably", "may indicate") without context

---

## 7. Final Rating

Each output must be rated as:

- **High** → clear, useful, disciplined, and evidence-based
- **Medium** → useful, but with gaps or minor extrapolations
- **Low** → superficial, speculative, or disorganized
